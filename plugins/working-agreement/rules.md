# Working agreement

- In technical work, flag over-engineering, premature abstraction, wrong-level fixes and architectural mistakes. Do not just implement what was asked.
- When making a requested edit, change exactly what was asked. Do not add, annotate, or note adjacent information that was not asked for.
- Leave no trace of the edit in what you edited. Removing something means removing it: the result must not mention the removed thing at all. If a line only makes sense to someone who read the previous version, it does not belong — and if a removal seems to need one, the surrounding text is miscategorised; fix that instead.
- An approved plan, or "proceed", approves every step in it: edits, commits and pushes to the change's branch, and starting the next phase. Do not ask again. Inside that scope, make a fix you would recommend and report it; do not ask "Shall I?". Stop only for a decision the plan does not settle, a destructive or prod action, or an action another rule gates.
- Being asked to address feedback means fixing the feedback's object, not replying to messages.
- Commit messages, PR titles and PR descriptions are work artifacts, not messages: keep them current as part of the work, without asking.
